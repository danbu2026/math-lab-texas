What is Little Math Lab?

A growing K–5 learning system for short, meaningful practice. Children solve, explain, test, and revisit ideas instead of racing through repetitive worksheets.

10 useful minutes
🎯

The purpose

Build strong mathematical understanding, reasoning, spatial thinking, and problem-solving habits that support school learning and future study.

🧠

How learning works

Check what the child knows, teach one idea, apply it in a new situation, then use a later Fresh Check to see what truly stayed.

🧭

Levels, not age labels

Children move by demonstrated readiness. A Level is a challenge to master, not a limit based on age or school grade.

⭐

Motivation with meaning

Completed released missions earn credits toward family-chosen rewards. There are no rankings, streak penalties, or pressure to stay online.

THE LEARNING FOUNDATION

One original system, built from complementary strengths

No single program does everything well. Little Math Lab combines proven learning principles while creating its own lessons, visuals, interactions, and feedback. It does not copy another company’s questions or course.

1

Concrete → visual → symbolic

Children first move, group, draw, rotate, or compare. Symbols and efficient procedures come after the relationship is visible.

2

Mastery with evidence

Speed and one correct answer are not enough. The system separates concept, procedure, application, and explanation evidence.

3

Productive struggle

Children predict and try before seeing a method. Hints move from a small question to a first step, then fade away.

4

Problem solving and transfer

New numbers, new stories, error analysis, and open reasoning show whether a child can use an idea beyond the example.

5

Retrieval over time

A later Fresh Check asks for the idea again without the earlier support. Prompted success is recorded as progress, not independent mastery.

6

Short, focused practice

Each mission has one purpose and may end early when the evidence is clear. The system does not add filler just to keep a child online.

WHAT THE CURRICULUM CONSIDERS

Knowledge, independent application, and reasoning

These three lenses serve different purposes. We use them together without turning the product into a test-prep simulator.

FOUNDATION

Texas TEKS + local sequence

Defines the K–5 mathematics children should learn, the prerequisites, common misconceptions, and evidence needed before moving on.

APPLICATION

Iowa / ITBS-informed skills

These are achievement assessments. This lens strengthens reading a problem, choosing operations, accurate work, graphs and data, multi-step application, and reasonable answers.

REASONING

CogAT-informed domains

CogAT is a reasoning-abilities assessment. This lens builds quantitative, visual-spatial, and verbal reasoning through patterns, analogies, classification, rotation, elimination, and proof.

Clear boundary: The project creates original learning tasks. It does not copy protected test questions, predict scores or placement, or promise a particular test result.

AI WITH A CLEAR JOB

AI supports the system; it does not do the thinking for the child

Where AI can help

  • Map a large curriculum and identify missing links.
  • Create and check varied practice candidates.
  • Use learning evidence to recommend the next useful mission.
  • Offer a smaller hint or another explanation when a child is stuck.
  • Help reviewers find unclear wording, math errors, and technical problems.

Where humans stay responsible

  • Children still predict, manipulate, solve, and explain.
  • Every released learning path receives human and independent Agent review.
  • Parents see what is ready, under review, or only planned.
  • AI output is not accepted as educational truth without checking.
  • The system never treats a fast click or hinted answer as mastery.

Current reality: AI assists curriculum design and quality review today. Automatic personal recommendations and live AI tutoring are planned capabilities, not finished features.

HOW YOUR CHILD USES IT

One mission at a time

  1. 1Choose Ella or Mila. Each learner keeps a separate path and record.
  2. 2Open Today. Start the next available ten-minute mission.
  3. 3Let the child think first. Hints help, but independent work is recorded separately.
  4. 4Ask for an explanation. “How do you know?” matters as much as the final answer.
  5. 5Stop when the mission is done. Short, focused use is the design—not endless screen time.

THE PARENT’S ROLE

  • Ask what changed, what stayed the same, and why.
  • Use the Progress page to see completed work.
  • Choose rewards together and keep them meaningful.
  • Tell us when instructions confuse the child—that is product evidence, not failure.
