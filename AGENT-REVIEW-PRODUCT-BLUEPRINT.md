# Independent Agent Review · Product Curriculum Blueprint v1.0

**Date:** 2026-10-03  
**Method:** Independent read-only review of the blueprint, README, current product claims, Carroll ISD official pages, and TEA mathematics standards.  
**Result:** PASS WITH REQUIRED CHANGES

## Overall judgment

The direction matches the family goal: support advanced learners, detect hidden gaps, emphasize understanding and transfer, and avoid repetitive test drilling. Version 1.0 was not yet safe for large-scale course production because several requirements remained directional rather than executable.

## Required changes identified

1. Replace broad topic lists with a verifiable K–5 standards coverage matrix.
2. Record separate evidence for concept understanding, procedure, application, and explanation/transfer.
3. Treat the children's reported Grade 3 and Grade 5 levels as starting hypotheses, not proof that whole grades are mastered.
4. Define diagnostic branching, prerequisite look-back, delayed retesting, skipping, and stopping rules.
5. Treat 360 as optional annual capacity, not a task quota or mastery promise.
6. Rename test-linked content as independent academic application and reasoning; do not imply score prediction or test validity.
7. Reconcile the existing 12 × 30 STEAM roadmap with the new personalized mathematics path.
8. Use staged release labels: prototype, content reviewed, ready for family test, family validated.
9. Build and test one 3–5 mission learning chain before expanding to 20 missions.
10. Add missing or unclear K–5 coverage, including required fraction work, operation order, prime/composite, data displays, and personal financial literacy details.
11. Preserve the limitation that the PWA has not received formal physical-iPad validation.
12. Provide an exit path for learners who demonstrate stable K–5 mastery.

## Review of the twelve blueprint questions

1. Family goal: substantially reflected; starting levels needed qualification.
2. TEKS/Iowa/CogAT: conceptually distinct; implementation boundaries needed strengthening.
3. K–5 scope: omissions and enrichment/required-standard boundaries needed correction.
4. Advanced learners: direction appropriate; diagnostic rules were not yet executable.
5. Ten minutes: supports a small learning loop; complex concepts must be allowed to span sessions.
6. Drill pressure: low, but test labels risked turning the system into exam categories.
7. 360 model: useful as capacity, not as a fixed annual requirement.
8. Current status: generally honest; roadmap language and iPad limitations needed reconciliation.
9. Release standards: strong start; required measurable evidence and staged release states.
10. Development order: full index first is sound, but one complete 3–5 lesson chain should precede 20 lessons.
11. Required before scale: coverage matrix, mastery rules, diagnostic rules, test boundaries, status levels, child starting plans, and blueprint consistency.
12. Final result: PASS WITH REQUIRED CHANGES.

## Resolution

The required changes were incorporated into Product & Curriculum Blueprint v1.1. A second independent review is required before the blueprint is treated as the production baseline.

# Second Independent Review · Blueprint v1.1

**Result:** PASS

Version 1.1 is approved to support the next stage: build the item-by-item K–5 standards coverage matrix and produce the first 3–5 mission learning chain. This approval does not mean the complete curriculum, adaptive diagnosis, or learning effectiveness has been validated.

## Confirmed corrections

- The children's advanced grade estimates are starting hypotheses, not proof of whole-grade mastery.
- TEKS content, independent academic application, and reasoning are clearly separated.
- Original activities cannot claim official scores, percentiles, placement predictions, or assessment validity.
- The broad K–5 scope is sufficient to begin item-by-item standards verification.
- Concept, procedure, application, and explanation/transfer evidence are recorded separately.
- Prompted success is not treated as independent mastery.
- Diagnostic look-back, delayed retesting, skipping, and advanced-learning exits are defined well enough for small-scale trials.
- Ten-minute missions may end early or continue across sessions.
- 360 is annual capacity, while the old website structure is identified as pending replacement.
- The development sequence begins with a 3–5 mission learning chain before expansion to 20 missions.
- Release stages and current product limitations are stated honestly.

## Implementation notes for the next stage

1. Verify every TEKS item in the matrix; do not treat the broad topic directory as the final checklist.
2. Define concrete evidence rules for the first learning chain, including prompts, retesting, skipping, accidental taps, and reading load.
3. Allow a learner to continue while an earlier skill is waiting for delayed retesting.
4. Update the website and README to the new personalized pathway before presenting that pathway as live.
5. Family validation applies only to the tested family context and does not establish general educational effectiveness.

**Final decision:** No unresolved issue blocks the standards-matrix stage or the first 3–5 mission learning chain.
